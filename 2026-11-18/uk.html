UK inflation rises to 3.6%, driven in part by fuel prices

Britain’s Consumer Prices Index rose 3.6% in the year to October, up from 3.5% in September, according to the Office for National Statistics. Petrol and diesel prices made an important contribution. The higher headline figure does not imply that all goods and services are becoming more expensive at the same rate.

For households, fuel and energy costs transmit into transport, heating and eventually business prices. For the Bank of England, a renewed inflation rise complicates decisions on interest rates at a time when some consumers and borrowers are already under pressure. The distinction between volatile energy inflation and more persistent underlying pressures will matter for future policy.

United KingdomPublic servicesStory 2 of 7

The NHS plans to leave Palantir’s data platform—but not until 2029

The UK government says Palantir’s role supplying the NHS Federated Data Platform will end in March 2029, rather than exercising a break option that might have brought the contract to an end earlier. Ministers argue a transition and new procurement need time; critics question why the supplier should remain so embedded for longer. Novara Media has reported on resistance among NHS staff and on whether hospitals cited as success stories are actually using the platform extensively.

This is a case study in the difficulty of changing technology suppliers in public services. Procurement, dependence on proprietary systems, claims of operational benefits and trust in health-data governance all have to be assessed separately. An announced end date is not evidence that a replacement is ready, and criticism of the vendor does not on its own establish what the existing platform has or has not achieved.

United KingdomSocietyStory 3 of 7

Researchers will evaluate the UK social-media restrictions affecting under-16s

Wellcome is funding ten research teams with £9.3 million to study the effects of forthcoming social-media restrictions on more than 300,000 young people. The projects will examine mental health, sleep, relationships and other outcomes, with some studies comparing behaviour before and after the changes.

The policy debate has often moved faster than the evidence. Researchers need to understand benefits, unintended effects, differences between groups and whether restrictions displace use into less visible spaces. This announcement is a study launch, not evidence that the ban works or fails; its value will lie in the quality of the resulting comparisons.

United KingdomPrivacyStory 4 of 7

NHS patient-record snooping incidents expose a distinct privacy problem

Freedom-of-information responses documented more than 3,000 confirmed occasions of NHS staff improperly accessing patient records across several years. Not every trust supplied complete comparable figures, so the count is not a national total of all misconduct. Some cases involve staff accessing records without a legitimate medical reason.

Healthcare data security is about internal access governance as much as external hackers or technology suppliers. Systems need effective audit trails, detection, sanctions and clear patient remedies. This is related to wider public trust in digitised health services, but is not proof of wrongdoing by any particular outside software vendor.

United KingdomBrexit · UK–EU relationsStory 5 of 7

Labour faces renewed pressure to define Britain’s long-term relationship with the EU

Former foreign secretary David Lammy has called on Prime Minister Andy Burnham to campaign at the next election on a commitment to rejoin the European Union. The intervention follows Burnham’s criticism of Brexit’s economic and diplomatic consequences, but his government has delayed setting out specific long-term options while preparing for a major UK–EU summit. Lammy’s position is a political proposal, not an adopted government policy.

The bigger question is whether Britain can deepen economic integration with the EU without reopening the political disputes that shaped the original Brexit negotiations. Trade barriers, standards, migration rules and access to European programmes affect businesses and households, but full membership would involve broader obligations and renewed democratic debate. Divisions within Labour, competing priorities in Brussels and concerns among Leave-supporting constituencies make any shift far from automatic.

United KingdomLocal government · AccountabilityStory 6 of 7

Reform’s promised local-government efficiency drive has yielded limited savings

A Guardian examination of English councils led by Reform UK reports that the party’s promised cost-cutting drive has not, so far, produced faster or deeper savings than those achieved by comparable authorities. The party had promoted a programme modelled on Elon Musk’s US government-efficiency initiative after its local-election gains. Interviews and comparative evidence suggest a gap between the rhetoric of immediate administrative overhaul and what councils have actually delivered.

The story matters because local-government budgets are constrained by statutory obligations, service demand and existing contracts. Reducing expenditure is not simply a matter of removing jobs or announcing targets: cuts can shift costs, reduce service quality or take longer to implement than promised. A short early performance period does not establish what may happen throughout a full council term, but the available results provide a test of a specific political claim about rapid savings.

United KingdomWork · Social policyStory 7 of 7

A sweeping review of youth employment proposes deep changes to welfare and training

A review led by Alan Milburn proposes wide-ranging measures to address the UK’s large population of young people not in education, employment or training (NEET). Recommendations include expanded apprenticeships, more specialised career support and wider changes across schools, health services and benefits. The review also contemplates substantial changes to disability-related support, prompting concern from critics about potential losses of financial security.

The larger issue is whether unemployment among young people is best addressed through skills and hiring incentives, conditional welfare reform, or stronger support for health, housing and access to work. The report recognises structural barriers such as poverty and ill health but couples new opportunities with tougher conditions. Its recommendations are not enacted policy. The government’s response—and independent assessment of potential effects on disabled and low-income young people—will matter more than the review’s scale alone.
