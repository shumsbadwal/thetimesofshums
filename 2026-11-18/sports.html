India prepare for Wellington Test as New Zealand defend top World Test Championship position

India begin a two-Test series in Wellington on Thursday, 19 November, against New Zealand, who lead the World Test Championship standings entering a demanding run of eight Tests. New Zealand captain Tom Latham confirmed the home line-up, including returning batters Devon Conway, Rachin Ravindra and Tom Blundell and a four-man seam attack. India are expected to welcome back Jasprit Bumrah, but selection and conditions will matter.

New Zealand have been a difficult opponent for India in their own conditions. With World Test Championship points at stake, a green Basin Reserve surface and unsettled batting combinations can be more consequential than pre-series rankings. This is a preview: no result from the first Test was available on the edition date.

Indian world champion D. Gukesh will defend his title against Uzbekistan's Javokhir Sindarov in Geneva. FIDE's updated schedule lists an opening ceremony on 22 November and the first of up to 14 classical games on 24 November. The match tests the preparation, stamina and nerves of two young elite players rather than a one-day knockout result. Opening theory, time management and decisions to take or avoid risks across fourteen games will matter. Earlier provisional dates differed, so the latest schedule from FIDE is the appropriate reference, and there is no match score yet.

Middle East conflict forces sports organisers to reconsider major events

The war involving Iran and regional disruption have prompted cancellations, postponements and relocations of high-profile events in the Middle East, according to Associated Press reporting. Affected competitions have included Formula 1 races earlier in the season, with events originally intended for Saudi Arabia also moved elsewhere. The commercial consequences extend to broadcasters, sponsors, teams and host cities.

International sport depends on reliable travel, insurance, public safety and long-term hosting commitments. Organisers face a tension between the commercial appeal of major-event investment and the risks of regional conflict. A relocation should not be treated as evidence that all sporting activity in the region has ceased.
