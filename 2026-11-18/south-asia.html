Bangladesh deploys paramilitary forces amid violence over Hasina’s planned return

Bangladesh deployed hundreds of paramilitary personnel on 18 November as violence intensified ahead of former prime minister Sheikh Hasina’s stated intention to return from exile in India in December. Reuters reported crude-bomb explosions and arson incidents across numerous districts, with people killed or injured in separate attacks. The government has declined her request for new identity and travel documents, while her banned Awami League has called for protests, including a planned nationwide shutdown.

The upheaval exposes how unsettled Bangladesh’s post-2024 political order remains. Hasina’s attempted return is an immediate trigger, but disputes about political legitimacy, prosecutions and the right to organise run deeper. Government officials blame overseas party figures for encouraging violence; these are allegations, not proof of responsibility for every incident. The next test is whether security operations can protect civilians without further narrowing political space. India also has a direct diplomatic stake because Hasina has been living there.

South AsiaBangladesh · Labour and exportsStory 2 of 9

Bangladesh’s garment workers press for higher wages as factories shut

Workers in Bangladesh’s export-oriented ready-made garment industry have continued demonstrations across Savar, Ashulia and Gazipur, demanding a minimum monthly wage of Tk 30,000. The Daily Star reported clashes with police, road blockades and disruptions to factory production. One report estimated that close to 450 factories faced a production halt on 17 November; industrial police said 25 factories in the Savar–Ashulia area were not operating on 18 November. Those counts cover different locations and reporting periods, not a single national total.

The bigger picture is a dispute about how the economic returns from Bangladesh’s garment exports are shared. The sector is central to export earnings and millions of livelihoods, but manufacturers also face energy costs and pressure from overseas buyers. Parliamentarians have alleged political orchestration of the unrest, while workers and labour organisations have raised wages and working conditions. Those are distinct claims: allegations about who may be exploiting protests do not establish that workers’ wage demands are illegitimate. The test is whether credible negotiations can address pay, workplace safety and the viability of factories without suppressing lawful organising.

South AsiaBangladesh / India · Water diplomacyStory 3 of 9

India and Bangladesh approach the expiry of their Ganges water-sharing treaty without formal renewal talks

The 30-year Ganges Water Sharing Treaty, signed in 1996, is scheduled to expire on 12 December. ThePrint reports that Indian water officials plan to visit Dhaka for a 21–23 November joint-committee meeting, but that renewal is not on the official agenda. Bangladeshi officials say they are ready to negotiate and want a revised agreement protecting dry-season flows. There has not yet been a mutually announced replacement agreement.

The bigger picture extends beyond a diplomatic deadline. The treaty establishes how water passing the Farakka Barrage is shared during the January–May dry season, with consequences for farming, river ecosystems, navigation and communities downstream. Bangladesh wants firmer assurances during low-flow periods; Indian political actors also cite the requirements of states such as Bihar. The challenge is reconciling competing water needs in a climate- and season-sensitive river basin. Routine technical committee meetings should not be mistaken for completed political negotiations on a new treaty.

South AsiaPakistan · Energy and regional tradeStory 4 of 9

A Saudi–Pakistan $30 billion energy proposal could reshape regional gas links—but is not a committed investment

Saudi company Delta Energy Saudi and Pakistan’s Inter State Gas Systems have agreed to evaluate an energy-infrastructure programme with a proposed value of about $30 billion. Concepts under consideration include pipelines connecting western Pakistan with Gwadar, LNG and storage infrastructure, and a possible corridor across Pakistan toward the Indian border. Afghan gas resources have also been mentioned as possible supply sources. The agreement sets up a feasibility process; it is not a final investment decision or guaranteed financing.

The bigger picture is South Asia’s difficult search for dependable, affordable energy. A cross-border gas network could link producers and consumers across Afghanistan, Pakistan and potentially India, but security, political relationships, financing and commercial demand have stalled earlier pipeline ambitions. A proposed route is not evidence that the governments concerned have agreed to build or use it. What matters next is feasibility work, credible financing, relevant permissions and any bilateral agreements needed to cross borders.

South AsiaPakistan · MacroeconomicsStory 5 of 9

Pakistan records a small current-account surplus, but external financing risks persist

Pakistan’s current account posted a $39 million surplus in October after a $371 million deficit the previous month, according to State Bank of Pakistan data reported by Dawn. The first four months of the fiscal year still recorded a cumulative $850 million current-account deficit. Foreign direct investment during July–October rose 22% year-on-year to $935 million, while the overall trade deficit widened.

The bigger picture is that a single monthly surplus is welcome but does not eliminate Pakistan’s external vulnerability. Import costs, energy prices, remittances, debt payments and the availability of investment can all shift the balance of payments quickly. The Gulf conflict makes energy and financial conditions particularly uncertain. A durable improvement would require sustained export competitiveness and investment rather than depending only on temporary swings in imports or dollar inflows.

South AsiaNepal / China · Trade and connectivityStory 6 of 9

Nepal asks China to reopen a crucial border crossing after flood damage

Nepal’s foreign minister Shisir Khanal urged a visiting Chinese delegation to press for the reopening of the Tatopani border crossing. The Rasuwagadhi route remains shut after severe floods in August, while several higher-altitude crossings become difficult to use during winter. Chinese representatives said they would take the request to the relevant authorities; the crossing had not yet been confirmed reopened at the time of reporting.

The bigger picture is the vulnerability of landlocked Nepal’s trade corridors to weather and geography. When one of a small number of border routes is disrupted, the consequences can spread to imports, logistics costs, local employment and wider relations with neighbouring countries. Reopening Tatopani is both a practical trade issue and a diplomatic test of cross-border coordination following climate-related damage. The longer-term problem is building a more resilient network rather than depending on one operational crossing.

South AsiaNepal · Disaster recoveryStory 7 of 9

Displaced Nepali families occupy public land as flood rehabilitation stalls

More than 250 families displaced by the 26 August Bhotekoshi floods occupied Nepal Electricity Authority land in Trishuli Bazar and started erecting temporary shelters. The Kathmandu Post reported that about 450 families in the town lost their homes completely, with hundreds more suffering damage. Residents say a delay in formal rehabilitation decisions left them without an adequate alternative.

The bigger picture is that disaster risk is not measured only by the number of buildings destroyed on the day of a flood. Recovery depends on land rights, funding, housing decisions and coordination among different levels of government. Families moving onto public land may gain temporary shelter while also creating new legal and safety disputes. The question is whether local and federal authorities can provide fair, lasting resettlement before temporary displacement becomes entrenched.

South AsiaSri Lanka / Bangladesh · Regional tradeStory 8 of 9

Sri Lanka proposes reviving a trade arrangement with Bangladesh

Sri Lanka’s foreign minister Vijitha Herath proposed reviving talks over a preferential trade agreement with Bangladesh in a meeting with Bangladeshi officials in Colombo. The proposal aims to encourage bilateral trade and economic cooperation. It is a diplomatic initiative at this stage; no completed agreement or tariff concessions have been announced.

The bigger picture is how much of South Asia’s trade potential remains constrained by political frictions, logistics costs and fragmented market access. Preferential agreements may create opportunities for businesses, but the effects depend on which goods are covered, how rules of origin work, and whether ports and customs procedures make trade economical. The important next step is whether discussions lead to a negotiated text and measurable reductions in trading barriers.

South AsiaAfghanistan · Humanitarian crisisStory 9 of 9

Afghanistan receives emergency UN funding as a difficult winter approaches

The UN approved $18 million in emergency funding intended to help more than 824,000 people in Afghanistan, according to the regional outlet Khaama Press citing OCHA. The support will cover food, health, nutrition, protection, shelter, water and sanitation. It forms part of a broader $48 million package, including $30 million from the Afghanistan Humanitarian Fund. The UN estimates that 21.9 million Afghans need humanitarian assistance in 2026.

The bigger picture is an enduring emergency shaped by food insecurity, poverty, people returning from neighbouring countries and the limited resources available to aid providers. Winter can intensify needs just as access to remote communities becomes more difficult. Emergency allocations may prevent suffering, but they do not solve long-term economic constraints or make up for the broader aid shortfall. The amounts are approved funding and planned reach, not proof that every intended recipient has already received assistance.
