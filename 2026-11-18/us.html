Democrats secure a 56–44 US Senate majority after Alaska result

Mary Peltola defeated Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan in Alaska, completing the Democrats’ ninth Republican-held Senate pickup of the 2026 midterms. The incoming Senate is set to have a 56–44 Democratic majority. Democrats have also won control of the House, sharply changing the balance of power in Congress.

The result constrains President Trump’s legislative agenda and creates more opportunities for congressional oversight, but it does not transfer executive authority to Congress. Control of committees, spending measures and subpoena power will matter. The figures describe the incoming Congress, which takes office in January, not the chamber’s composition on election night.

United StatesInstitutionsStory 2 of 6

Congressional investigations of Trump may face constitutional barriers

Democrats preparing to control congressional committees have said they will pursue broad investigations of President Trump and his administration. Reuters examined how Supreme Court precedents on congressional subpoenas, presidential records and separation of powers might affect those efforts. Congress can seek documents and testimony, but legal enforcement can be slow and contested.

The key question is whether the incoming majority can exercise oversight effectively when the executive branch resists. Political control of a committee does not automatically guarantee access to every record; equally, the existence of legal limits does not negate Congress’s investigative powers. The story is about institutional checks, not a completed finding of misconduct.

A $50bn air-traffic funding request meets resistance after mass flight disruption

The Trump administration is seeking roughly $50 billion for air-traffic-control reforms and airport improvements after a nationwide ground stop caused more than 18,000 flight cancellations. The request expanded from a $30 billion proposal, and some Republican senators have challenged the scale and mix of spending.

The aviation system has long-running modernisation problems, but a high-profile failure is not enough to settle which investments are most effective. Lawmakers want accountability for earlier funding and clearer priorities. The distinction between urgent safety improvements and broader airport upgrades will shape the debate over who pays and how quickly repairs can be made.

Appeals court blocks removal of transgender service members while litigation continues

A divided federal appeals court blocked the US military from forcing more than two dozen transgender service members out while litigation over the Trump administration’s policy continues. The ruling follows earlier findings that the policy was unlawfully discriminatory; the administration is seeking further Supreme Court review. The order concerns the affected personnel and ongoing proceedings, not a final nationwide resolution of every challenge to military service rules.

The case tests the extent to which the executive branch can set personnel standards for the armed forces while remaining subject to constitutional protections. Supporters of the policy frame it as a question of military readiness and administrative discretion; challengers say exclusion based on gender identity is discriminatory. The court’s interim protection preserves the status of the service members involved while the merits and procedural questions continue to be contested.

United StatesCourts · Academic freedomStory 5 of 6

Supreme Court leaves in place a ruling against cancellation of university research grants

The US Supreme Court declined to hear the Trump administration’s appeal concerning the cancellation of about $59 million in grants to researchers at the University of California, Berkeley. The Ninth Circuit had found that the grant terminations were likely unlawful viewpoint discrimination because they targeted work described as supporting diversity, equity and inclusion. The research covered fields including medicine, climate policy, water management and the humanities.

The significance lies in the boundary between government power to set funding priorities and constitutional limits on penalising disfavoured viewpoints. Declining review leaves the lower-court ruling in place; it is not a fresh Supreme Court ruling deciding all disputes about DEI-related funding. Academic institutions and federal agencies will be watching how this reasoning affects other grant challenges and the conditions under which scientific work is financed.

United StatesInfrastructure · Digital resilienceStory 6 of 6

US officials say recent major technology outages are not known to be connected

A succession of disruptions affecting air travel, banking, online services and government communications has prompted public speculation about a coordinated cyberattack. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said officials had no evidence linking the incidents to one another or to a malicious actor. The organisations involved have attributed various failures to technical problems. Those statements reflect the available evidence, not proof that future investigations cannot uncover additional causes.

The bigger story is the fragility of highly interconnected services. A problem in one platform or update can interfere with critical activities far beyond the original system. Similar-looking failures may be unrelated, and treating coincidence as proof of sabotage can distract from engineering, maintenance and transparency. The useful follow-up is whether operators publish credible root-cause analyses and what they change to prevent repeat outages—not whether a dramatic common explanation circulates online.
