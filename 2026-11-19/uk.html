Inflation reaches 3.6% as Bank of England weighs another increase

UK annual consumer-price inflation rose from 3.5% in September to 3.6% in October, according to the Office for National Statistics as reported by AP and Reuters. Energy costs are an important driver; the Bank of England has recently raised its policy rate to 4%.

The inflation figure is a released observation, while a further December rate increase remains a market expectation rather than a decision. Higher borrowing costs and energy bills sharpen the political trade-off between controlling inflation and protecting household incomes.

British authorities transfer detained US soldier to American custody

Reuters reported that UK authorities transferred a serving US Army officer arrested near Gloucester on 10 November in a national-security investigation to US military custody.

An investigation and custody transfer do not establish guilt. The cross-border handling illustrates how allied security and criminal procedures can overlap.

United KingdomReportingStory 3 of 4

Underlying inflation eases pressure for an immediate December rate rise

October UK headline inflation reached 3.6%, but core CPI remained at 2.8% and services inflation at 3.7%, both softer than forecasters expected. Reuters reported that market-implied chances of a December Bank of England rate rise slipped from about 90% to 80%.

The difference between headline and underlying measures matters: imported energy costs can lift consumer prices without proving an entrenched domestic wage-price spiral. The market probability is not an official Bank decision.

Scotch whisky tariff dispute tests the new UK–India trade agreement

The British government said ministers were urgently working with India after Reuters reported an effective 162.5% duty on some bulk Scotch whisky imports. The July trade agreement had reduced the agreed tariff on Scotch to 75%, but Indian customs investigators allege incorrect classifications by some importers.

The dispute concerns the practical application of a negotiated tariff concession, not necessarily a formal withdrawal of the trade pact. Exporters' commercial impact depends on how customs classifications and assessments are resolved.
