Shenzhen summit tests the limits of three-power diplomacy

Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin criticised protectionism and sanctions ahead of expected talks with Donald Trump in Shenzhen. Ukraine, Iran, trade, AI and nuclear weapons are on the proposed agenda. The meeting was still prospective at this edition cutoff: no agreement or verifiable concession can be inferred from the gathering.

The participants have conflicting strategic interests, and governments affected by their decisions are not all at the table. The significance will lie in documented commitments and implementation rather than summit imagery.

Myanmar genocide case approaches a consequential ICJ ruling

Reuters reports that the International Court of Justice is due to rule on whether Myanmar's 2017 military campaign against the Rohingya constituted genocide. This is a scheduled judgment, not a decision already delivered.

The court's reasoning may matter for how state responsibility under the Genocide Convention is assessed in other conflicts. The outcome must be reported only after publication of the judgment.

Nuclear-arms treaty vacuum shadows the Shenzhen meeting

The US, China and Russia enter the Shenzhen summit without a replacement for the expired New START treaty. AP reports that China sees little incentive to accept numerical restrictions while its arsenal remains smaller than those of the US and Russia.

The practical question is whether the leaders can agree on verifiable risk-reduction measures, rather than simply promising talks. Ukraine and European governments have voiced concerns about negotiations affecting them without their participation.
