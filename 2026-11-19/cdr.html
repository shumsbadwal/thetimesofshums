Norwegian mesocosm study tests OAE impacts on zooplankton

A peer-reviewed ICES Journal of Marine Science paper published 17 November tested lime- and silicate-inspired ocean alkalinity enhancement in ten Norwegian fjord mesocosms, spanning additions up to 600 µmol/kg and pH around 8.1–8.7.

The 54-day experiment reported limited OAE effects on overall food-web dynamics, while zooplankton biomass declined across experimental phases. This is a constrained ecological experiment, not evidence of independently verified atmospheric CO2 removal or full-scale deployment safety.

Marine removal crediting must distinguish chemical potential from delivered tonnes

The OAE study evaluates biological responses to chemical manipulation; it does not establish net atmospheric CO2 uptake, permanent storage, project MRV or credit issuance.

Any commercial claim needs baseline chemistry, measured alkalinity, air–sea equilibration, lifecycle emissions, leakage and ecological monitoring. Planned, modelled, contracted, delivered and independently verified removals are separate accounting states.
