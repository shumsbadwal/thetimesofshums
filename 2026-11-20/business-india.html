Indian equities rally against regional market weakness

The Nifty 50 rose 1.14% to 25,514.30 and the Sensex gained 1.21% to 83,021.75 on Thursday. Autos and banks led gains while IT declined; Reuters linked sentiment to lower commodity prices and renewed foreign inflows. The numbers are closing-market observations, not guarantees of further gains.

RBI intervention recedes as capital returns

Reuters reported RBI net dollar sales including forward maturities of about $4.8 billion from 15 October to 6 November, versus $48 billion in the prior month. Stronger inflows and cheaper oil eased rupee pressure, but reserve and forward-book management remain important uncertainties.

Airlines plan fewer domestic flights for November–December

An Indian Express analysis of Cirium schedules indicated 7,154 fewer domestic flights than the corresponding period last year, a 3.5% fall in scheduled flights. These are published schedules rather than verified flights operated, and may change with cancellations and revisions.

India's foreign-exchange reserves drop $14.2 billion in one week

Reserve Bank of India data published Friday showed foreign-exchange reserves at $705.342 billion for the week ended 13 November, down from $719.547 billion a week earlier. Foreign-currency assets and gold holdings accounted for most of the reported decline.

The headline change should not be interpreted as an equivalent amount of spot-market intervention. Valuation shifts and foreign-exchange swap maturities also affect the dollar value of reserves. The rupee had weakened during the reference week but strengthened to about 92.67 per dollar on Friday, illustrating the importance of dates when comparing currency and reserve figures.

SEBI consults on changes to governance of market infrastructure

India's securities regulator proposed changes to the tenure and age rules applying to leaders and independent directors of exchanges and other market infrastructure institutions. These bodies occupy a special position: they are commercial enterprises but also guardians of market integrity.

The proposals are part of a consultation process, not regulations already in force. The relevant trade-off is between continuity and expertise on one side and accountability, succession and conflicts of interest on the other. The final text and responses from exchanges will determine the practical effects.

Regulator proposes easing enhanced foreign-investor disclosures

SEBI proposed removing additional disclosure obligations introduced in 2023 for certain foreign portfolio investors. The measure is intended to reduce compliance burdens but also raises questions about transparency into beneficial ownership and concentrated investments.

It remains a proposal, not a repeal. A proper assessment needs to identify which investors would be affected, what disclosure requirements would remain and whether the change would weaken the ability to detect opaque ownership structures. Market access and investor protection need not always be opposing objectives, but their balance requires evidence.
