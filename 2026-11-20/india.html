Voter-roll deletions draw US rights criticism and Indian rebuttal

A US government religious-freedom commission accused India of discriminating against Muslim citizens in a nationwide electoral-roll revision. Reuters reported that approximately 130 million entries were removed. India's Election Commission says the exercise targets deceased, duplicate, relocated and otherwise ineligible voters and provides appeal mechanisms.

Deletion from a voter list is not proof of foreign nationality. The US commission's recommendations are nonbinding; India's foreign ministry rejected the criticism as politically biased. The most important unresolved questions are how many eligible citizens were wrongly excluded, whether appeals are effective and whether electoral-roll processes have been improperly connected to immigration enforcement.

Wheat planting faces possible sharp decline as farmers change crops

Industry estimates suggest Indian wheat acreage could decline by roughly 6% as drought and changing crop economics affect planting decisions. The estimate is a projection during the sowing season, not a completed agricultural census.

A sustained acreage fall could complicate grain procurement, food-price management and farmers' income expectations. Weather, irrigation access and government purchase prices will determine the eventual harvest more than one early forecast. The edition will not convert this preliminary estimate into a confirmed production shortfall.

Electoral-roll exclusions raise questions beyond administrative accuracy

A Reuters investigation documented families facing detention, deportation or citizenship questioning after names disappeared from electoral rolls. The reporting included West Bengal, where Muslim voters accounted for a disproportionate share of certain deletions. The Election Commission and the government maintain that the revision is an eligibility exercise.

The legal and democratic distinction is fundamental: being absent from a voter register does not establish a person's nationality. Cases under judicial challenge, individual testimonies, official statistics and government denials must be evaluated separately. The story's significance lies in due process and the ability of citizens to restore their electoral rights.
