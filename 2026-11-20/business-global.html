Global AI selloff sends Asian equities to their worst week since 2020

Global technology shares came under renewed pressure on Friday even after Anthropic's historic stock-market debut. Reuters reported the MSCI Asia-Pacific index excluding Japan was down about 8% for the week, its sharpest weekly decline since March 2020, while Taiwan's market fell even more steeply.

The immediate debate is whether immense AI infrastructure spending can translate into durable profits. Investors moved towards government bonds as equity volatility increased. These are market snapshots at the reporting cutoff; the economic question is broader than one company's IPO and includes financing costs, earnings expectations and concentration risk.

Oil market balances Gulf shipping risk against surplus expectations

Oil prices were little changed on 19 November as disruptions linked to the Gulf conflict offset concern about excess global supply. Prices can therefore remain elevated relative to peacetime assumptions even when traders anticipate sufficient barrels in aggregate.

The effects reach beyond energy companies: shipping insurance, airline fuel, inflation and import bills are exposed to the risk of maritime disruption. A quoted price is a point-in-time market observation, not a forecast. Subsequent moves depend on actual shipping flows, production decisions and the conflict's trajectory.
