Ocean alkalinity study finds mineral performance depends on dose

A September mesocosm study using northeastern Arabian Sea water found periclase increased alkalinity at modest dosing, while higher dosing triggered secondary precipitation and olivine dissolved slowly. It is experimental evidence about chemistry, not proof of atmospheric carbon removal at commercial scale.

Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth after 49 years

NASA announced that Voyager 1 reached a distance of one light-day from Earth on 18 November. Light requires 24 hours to cross that distance, approximately 25.9 billion kilometres, meaning even a radio message from the spacecraft takes a day to arrive.

The achievement is a communications and engineering milestone rather than a sudden new scientific discovery. Launched in 1977, Voyager 1 entered interstellar space in 2012 and continues to gather measurements beyond the heliosphere. Its ageing systems, limited power and very long signal delays make each successful scientific transmission remarkable.

Medical-device researchers examine implications of EU AI regulation

A paper published in npj Digital Medicine examines proposed changes to European medical-device regulation and their consequences for AI-enabled devices. The subject is important because software that adapts or relies on data-driven models can pose validation and oversight challenges distinct from conventional equipment.

This is an analysis of regulatory proposals, not evidence that any particular AI medical product is safe or effective. Clinicians and regulators must still evaluate clinical performance, monitoring after deployment, transparency, and whether changes to algorithms require fresh assessment. The article is a policy analysis rather than a clinical trial.
