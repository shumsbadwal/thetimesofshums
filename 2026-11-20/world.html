International court's Rohingya genocide ruling raises the question of enforcement

The International Court of Justice ruled on 19 November that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya during the military campaign of 2017. Myanmar rejected the judgment. The ruling is a landmark determination of state responsibility, but it is not itself a mechanism for safely repatriating refugees or ending the fighting inside Myanmar.

Roughly 1.2 million Rohingya are living in Bangladesh, according to Reuters reporting from Cox's Bazar. Refugees welcomed the ruling while confronting the absence of secure conditions for return. Bangladesh wants repatriation, but a credible path would require safety, rights, monitoring and meaningful political change inside Myanmar. The distinction between legal accountability and implementation is central to the story.

COP31 negotiations run past deadline as fossil-fuel dispute persists

Climate negotiators in Antalya were set to continue beyond Friday's scheduled conclusion after a draft agreement omitted explicit fossil-fuel transition language. Delegates also disagreed over adaptation finance, with a proposed quantified $120 billion annual target absent from the latest text.

This was a negotiating draft, not a final adopted agreement. Countries dependent on fossil-fuel revenues, vulnerable states demanding finance and governments seeking faster decarbonisation entered the final phase with different priorities. The ultimate measure of success will be what countries formally adopt and how they implement it.
