COP31 runs into overtime over fossil fuels and finance

Antalya negotiations passed Friday's planned deadline after a revised draft removed explicit fossil-fuel transition language and a proposed $120 billion adaptation-finance figure. Countries disagree over mitigation obligations and support for vulnerable economies. The draft is not a final COP decision; its eventual legal and political force depends on adopted wording.

Pacific states warn adaptation funding is falling short

The Pacific Resilience Facility was reported about $300 million short of a $500 million target despite new pledges. Island representatives say sea-level rise and flooding require accessible finance, not only future mitigation promises. A pledge is not necessarily disbursed money, and projected flooding frequencies are scenario-based rather than observed 2050 outcomes.

Copernicus expands El Niño observation tools

Copernicus released Climate Pulse 2.0 on November 10, adding separate ENSO-region temperature monitoring and historical comparisons. Its ERA5 reanalysis combines observations and modelling and typically lags real time by around two days. A warm single-day Niño 3.4 reading should not be confused with an officially classified multi-month ENSO index or proof of a specific local disaster.

Carbon-removal language at COP31 sparks dispute over fossil-fuel substitution

The revised draft encourages carbon-removal technologies while dropping explicit fossil-fuel language. Critics fear that speculative future removals could be used to justify continuing emissions. No new independently verified marine-CDR removal volume follows from this diplomatic text: planned, modelled, contracted and physically delivered removal are distinct categories.
