Bangladesh political violence tests fragile post-election settlement

Bangladesh saw arson, road disruption and deadly political clashes as supporters of exiled former prime minister Sheikh Hasina attempted a shutdown. Prime Minister Tarique Rahman blamed Awami League figures and suggested outside interference; the party disputes responsibility. Police reports, partisan allegations and independently established facts must be separated. Garment-worker wage protests add an economic dimension to the unrest.

Nepal's prime minister prepares first bilateral visit to India

Prime Minister Balendra Shah is due to meet Narendra Modi on Monday to discuss reconstruction after August floods, trade, energy and connectivity. Reuters reports the disaster killed around 1,500 and left thousands missing. The proposed visit is a diplomatic opportunity, not yet a signed reconstruction package or new air-route agreement.

Bangladesh parties plan competing street campaigns

The Daily Star reports the ruling BNP and opposition groups are organising separate programmes amid attacks on political offices and properties. The banned Awami League's role is disputed, and opposition parties are pressing their own constitutional and law-and-order demands. The immediate test is whether political competition can proceed without violence and collective punishment.
