Wall Street selloff pushes S&P 500 and Dow into corrections

Reuters reported the S&P 500 down 3.70%, Dow down 1.93% and Nasdaq down 5.28% on November 20. The S&P 500 and Dow were more than 10% below their record closes, the conventional correction threshold. The sharp move reflected anxiety about AI valuations and growth; market prices alone cannot identify a single cause.

Global technology unwind spreads into commodities and bonds

Reuters estimates nearly $9 trillion was erased from global equity values since August. Taiwan suffered a severe weekly decline, while government bonds rallied and copper weakened. The figure is a market-value comparison, not a cash loss realised by every investor; shifting expectations for US rates compounded AI concerns.

Eurozone consumer confidence improves modestly

The European Commission's flash November estimate rose one point to −16.9 from −17.9 in October; the EU-wide measure reached −16.5. Sentiment remains negative despite the improvement. This is a survey indicator, not a measurement of realised household spending.

Anthropic listing fails to stabilise AI-linked equities

Anthropic's shares fell 9.1% on their second trading day, according to Reuters, while Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta also weakened. The newly public model maker is being treated by investors as a proxy for the sector, but its price is not a direct measure of model quality or long-run AI productivity.
