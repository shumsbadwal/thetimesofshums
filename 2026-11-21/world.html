World Food Programme trims 2027 aid reach despite deepening hunger

The World Food Programme says its $12 billion appeal would reach 91 million people in 2027, down from a 110 million target this year, while it estimates 266 million people face acute or severe hunger. The gap is a funding constraint rather than evidence that need has fallen. The agency cites conflict, food prices and El Niño-related agricultural risks; the appeal is not yet secured.

Iran and United States remain divided over terms to end war

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said Tehran remains open to mediation but wants assurances against renewed conflict and to retain its enriched-uranium stockpile. President Trump said the terms offered were unacceptable. The IAEA's pre-war estimate of 440.9 kilograms enriched to 60% is historical: its current whereabouts and quantity are not verified. Diplomatic contact is not a ceasefire.

UNICEF says Ukraine child casualties approach first-war-year toll

UNICEF verified at least 114 children killed and 1,030 injured in Ukraine in January–October 2026, a total of 1,144 against 1,386 in all of 2022. The agency also cited roughly 270 attacks on educational facilities this year. These are documented casualties, not a complete estimate of every incident; Russia denies intentionally targeting civilians.

Indonesia and China revisit sensitive South China Sea cooperation

Presidents Prabowo and Xi discussed joint development and wider investment cooperation at APEC. Jakarta insists it does not recognise China's expansive maritime claims, a crucial qualification after earlier agreements sparked sovereignty concerns. The governments also described trade negotiations and business agreements, but announcements do not settle maritime boundaries.
