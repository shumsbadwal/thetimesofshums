COP31 ends without a consensus political agreement

Reuters reported that the Antalya climate summit closed after the EU and other delegations rejected a draft that did not adequately address fossil-fuel transition. The failure of the overarching decision does not mean every technical negotiation produced no outcome. Turkey's presidency denied claims of preferential access for Saudi Arabia.

Saudi influence and carbon-capture language come under scrutiny

Reuters reported negotiators' allegations that Saudi Arabia had unusually strong access during COP31 and opposed explicit fossil-fuel transition language while promoting carbon capture. The presidency disputed preferential-treatment claims. Carbon capture language is not evidence of delivered or independently verified carbon removal.

Pacific states and European negotiators explain COP31's breakdown

Delegates at COP31 in Antalya failed to agree on an overarching decision on 22 November. The Marshall Islands and European Union objected to language they considered too weak on fossil fuels, while Palau argued that even an imperfect draft could have advanced ocean, finance and electrification work. Their differing assessments demonstrate why the collapse of a political cover decision should not be equated with a total absence of technical progress.
