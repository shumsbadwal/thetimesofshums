COP31 negotiators near possible compromise after overtime talks

In Antalya, a revised COP31 package circulated after prolonged disagreement over fossil-fuel language and adaptation finance. At the Sunday-morning cutoff, the conference president expressed optimism, but final adoption had not been verified. A draft text, a proposed compromise and an adopted UN decision are different stages and must not be conflated.

Coalition pushes for explicit fossil-fuel transition in COP31 outcome

Reporting on 21 November described around 130 countries seeking stronger language on moving away from fossil fuels, opposed by Saudi Arabia and some other producers. Vulnerable countries also pressed for adaptation finance. Negotiating positions and reported coalition sizes are not evidence of an agreed phase-out timetable.

Copernicus projects another calendar year above 1.5°C

Copernicus' October press resources projected a 2026 global mean anomaly of 1.56–1.61°C above the 1850–1900 preindustrial reference, using a statistical model fitted to ERA5 reanalysis. This is a calendar-year forecast, not a final observed annual result or proof that the Paris Agreement's long-term temperature threshold has been formally crossed. Copernicus also reported a December 2025 rolling CO₂ average of 425.91 ppm; its baseline and time period differ from NOAA's Mauna Loa series.

Energy-security shock changes climate policy trade-offs

The IEA expects the Iran conflict to push governments toward more domestic renewable and nuclear energy, while some countries may also retain coal as backup. This is an outlook contingent on policies and investment, not a measured emissions reduction. For marine CDR, changing energy mix and ocean conditions remain relevant to lifecycle accounting, but no new verified removal tonnage is established by this report.
